00 — Introduction

Shaping Tomorrow
today

DCITC is the technology club of Dhaka College. It is a student run organization built around study groups, real projects, and the discipline of understanding systems before shipping them.

01 — History

From a classroom to a Community.

2024

The biggining

A group of students starts meeting weekly to share what they are learning in programming.

2025

Founded

Workshops become structured; the first lab machines arrive; the club grows past fifty members.

2026

Rebuilt as a study org

Study groups are founded; the club re-centers on fundamentals and public notes.

2025

First software ships

PadChat enters the lab, VoltLab is adopted by physics, CampusMesh covers two halls.

2026

Open source

Nodhini releases under MIT; 120+ members; the club runs its own infrastructure.

02 — Mission

Make fundamentals a habit.

Our mission is to give every member the ability to understand technology deeply enough to build with it — and the documentation skills to keep learning after they leave.

01 / STUDY Required Sessions
▸ fundamentals first ▸ public notes ▸ verified resources
02 / BUILD project teams
▸ real problems ▸ documented code
03 / TEACH everyone
▸ journal posts ▸ workshops

03 — Philosophy

What we believe about technology.

A / LEARN

Understand before you build

Code written without understanding the system underneath tends to break in ways nobody can explain. Taking the time to understand first means the work holds up under real conditions.

B / DEPTH

Tools should serve thinking, not replace it

Tools can move fast, but only careful thinking makes their output worth trusting. The goal is to use every tool available while still knowing what it did and why.

C / DEPTH

Depth matters more than speed

A working demo is not the same as real understanding, and shortcuts taken early tend to resurface as harder problems later. Depth may be slower to build, but it is the only kind of knowledge that lasts.

04 — How we work

A loop, not an event.

Study

A reading group picks one topic and works through it with public notes.

Build

The group proposes a small project that tests what it read.

Publish

Code, measurements and a write-up go to the repo and the journal.

→ the output of one loop becomes the reading of the next.

05 — Activities

What actually happens here.

ACT.01 Weekly study groups Six groups, one hour, zero slides. Notes are published after every session.
ACT.02 Project nights Quiet, scheduled work time in the lab with mentors available.
ACT.03 Workshops & talks Short, hands-on, and repeatable — the same material that made us, passed on.
ACT.04 Infra duty Members rotate keeping the club's mirrors, journal and lab machines alive.
ACT.05 Build Week Twice a year, 48 hours, real constraints. The closest thing we have to a tradition.
